# v0.2.0-p4.4 - Pull to Shoot

**Date:** 2026-10-24 · **Phase:** P2's last part, the controls tuned from the night. The user's notes after playing on phones: "Firing an arrow didn't feel consistent. It felt like I should have fired an arrow about 50% of the time, but no arrow was fired and I had ammo. Need to lock in the inputs and make sure arrows are fired intentionally. Let's change the firing to: when you put the right thumb down, you draw the bow, pulling outside the input circle, then releasing. This would cause an arrow to fire to the right, if you moved your thumb to the left. Replicate the arrow arch based off the angle of release." The user's picks: arrows lob from the bow, and "an indication that an arrow is guaranteed to fire when released". · **Harness:** the proofs below
**Report:** `bench/tools/input_probe.gd` (`_shots_lock_in`), `tools/v2_pull_check.py`, `bench/tools/bot_probe.gd`

> Every shot you mean now fires. Put your right thumb down to draw the bow, pull it away, and the arrow aims the other way. Past the ring it is armed: the ring turns gold and says "LET GO: ARROW!", and a gold arrow shows on your fighter. Let go and it always fires. Let go inside the ring and nothing fires, so you can back out. Arrows now fly in a real arc from the bow, so a long shot needs aiming up.

## Why shots went missing
A let-go shoot only fired if the bow was still up at that moment, and several things lowered it with no sign: a jump (on a phone, the move stick pushed up is a jump), a wall jump, a ledge grab, a dodge, a knock. A press that landed in a dodge or on a ledge never raised the bow at all. Each time, letting go did nothing.

## Changes
- **1. The shot locks in** (`sim/sim.gd`, `apply_input`). Holding shoot is a draw (`drawn`) that lasts through jumps, wall jumps, ledge grabs, dodges and knocks. The bow comes up whenever the fighter can (it stands still on the ground, as before). Letting go fires at once, or, in a dodge, a ledge hang or a knock, the first tick the fighter can, at the aim it was let go with (`shot_wait`, `shot_aim`; at most a dodge's 20 ticks). Only death drops it. The client's copy of your fighter carries both (`write_fighter_state`, 9 bytes more).
- **2. `B_CANCEL`** (`proto.gd`, the free bit 128): let go of shoot with it and the bow comes down with no arrow.
- **3. Pull back to shoot on a phone** (`client/touch_sticks.gd`). The thumb down anywhere on the right half draws the bow; pulling it away aims the opposite way; past the ring (110 px of the design, the knob follows to 210) the shot is armed. Letting go inside the ring sends `B_CANCEL`, kept on until the next touch so a skipped tick cannot lose it. A tap never fires.
- **4. The sign that a shot will fire.** Armed: the ring turns gold and thick with an arrow pointing where the shot goes, "LET GO: ARROW!" above it, and a bold gold arrow from your fighter's bow (`world.show_aim`). Not armed: a white ring, "PULL PAST THE RING", a thin pale line at the bow. Armed with an empty quiver: red, "NO ARROWS". The mouse on the PC is unchanged: held is armed.
- **5. Arrows lob** (`sim._update_arrow`; the bots' copy `brain.fly`). A flying arrow falls by `ARROW_LOB_G` (0.2 px a tick, every tick) from the moment it leaves the bow, with no drag, at `ARROW_SPEED` 16 px a tick (TowerFall's arrow, 18 px a tick, flew straight for 25 ticks first). At 45 degrees it reaches 1 280 px. A flat shot drops about half a body over 400 px.
- **6. The bots aim the arc** (`sim/brain.gd`). `lob_angles` gives the low and the high arc to the aim point; each is tried with small offsets and checked with the bots' copy of the arrow before they let go. They lower the bow with `B_CANCEL` instead of a jump, and any release they do not mean as a shot carries it (`want_shot`).
