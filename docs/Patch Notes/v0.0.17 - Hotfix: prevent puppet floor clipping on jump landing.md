# v0.0.17 - Hotfix: prevent puppet floor clipping on jump landing

**Date:** 2026-09-03 · **Phase:** Phase 3b hotfix · **Harness:** 17/17
**Report:** [harness_report_v0.0.17.json](../harness_report_v0.0.17.json)

> When a player landed on a platform and stood still, the other screens drew them sinking through the platform, then popping back up. Fixed: a landed puppet stays on its feet.

## The problem
A **puppet** is the other player's fighter as drawn on *your* screen. Your screen gets a new sample of where they are 20 times a second. To look smooth, it draws the puppet a tenth of a second late, between two samples.

When a sample is late, your screen makes a guess: "they are probably still moving the same way". That guess is allowed for up to a fifth of a second.

Here is what went wrong. The other player jumped, fell, and landed. Then they stood still. Standing still means "nothing changed", so their game stopped sending samples to save bandwidth. Your screen ran out of samples and made its guess: the last move was *falling*, so it kept drawing the puppet falling. Straight through the platform. A fall is about 500 pixels a second, and a fifth of a second of that is 100 pixels. The fighter is only 24 pixels tall. So the puppet sank four bodies deep, froze there, and popped back up half a second later when the "still here" repeat packet arrived.

The same guess also made a puppet slide a little past the spot where a player stopped running.

## What we did
Two small changes. One on the sending side, one on the drawing side.

1. **The packet now says "feet on the ground".** Every position packet has a byte of on/off flags (facing, dash, shield, bear, egg). We use one more bit: are the feet on the floor? On the drawing side, if the newest sample says "feet on the ground", the guess is not allowed to move the puppet up or down any more. Only sideways. So a landed puppet stays on the platform. A bonus: landing changes the flag, so the touchdown always sends a packet, even if the spot did not change.
2. **One "I stopped" packet.** When a player comes to rest, their game now repeats the last spot one more time, one tick (50 ms) later. Your screen then sees two samples in the same place and knows the speed is zero. No more guess at all. This costs one small 11-byte packet per stop.

3. **A puppet above the top edge is drawn where it really is.** While counting dips we found an older mistake in the drawing code. A fighter can really be above the top of the screen for a moment (a jump from the apex platform, a mage blink, a dash upward). The drawing code treated any spot above the top as "fell out of the bottom" and drew the puppet 386 pixels lower, near the floor. Now it only does that for a real crossing of the bottom seam (more than half a seam away). A fighter at y = -50 is drawn at y = -50, off the top, like the real one.

## How we know it is fixed
We taught the game to count **dips**: frames where a puppet is drawn below a sample that says "feet on the ground". The number shows up in the NetStats console line as `dips=`. The test harness now fails a run if it is above zero.

| Two wandering bots, 60 s | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| dip frames (puppet drawn under its floor) | 33 | 0 |
| jitter, mean px/s (Godot1 / Godot2) | 23.6 / 21.1 | 18.9 / 20.6 |

| Four fighting bots, 90 s | dips | jitter mean px/s | gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120 ± 40 ms (standard) | 0 | 23–29 | PASS |
| 120 ± 150 ms (heavy) | 0 | 59–71 | PASS |
| full test harness | — | — | 17/17 |

One standard-gate run in the middle of this work failed with two findings that were not the dips or the jitter (the run took 18 s longer than normal, so a client was probably slow to start). The run right after it passed. We did not catch the finding names, so it is written down here rather than hidden.

## Changes
- `scripts/global.gd`: new flag bit `FLAG_FLOOR` (32) in `sync_pos`; `encode_sync_pos` takes `on_floor`; the decoder gives `floor`; `dips` counter and `dips=` in the NetStats line.
- `scripts/player.gd`: sends `is_on_floor()` in every sample; repeats one packet when it comes to rest; `_extrapolate` keeps a "feet down" puppet level; counts dips; `_wrap_into_arena` only wraps a spot more than half a seam (193 px) above the top, not 10 px.
- `tools/chaos_bots.py`: knows the new flag (the fake bots set it when they rest), reads `dips=`, and fails a run with `*.puppet-dips` when it is above zero.
- The packet size did not change (still 11 bytes), so the server needed no change.

## Still open
- **A fighter can get stuck above the top edge.** Seen in the heavy test: a bot at x ≈ 181–190 or x ≈ 393, y ≈ -36 to -57, with its feet "on the ground", three things touching it, and being pushed up 17 px every tick while only moving sideways. There is no platform up there in the arena scene, and projectiles and the power-up are not solid. Not fixed here (it is the arena, not the netcode). Written in the PASSDOWN backlog.
- **Egg form runs on puppets (to check).** In `player.gd` the egg branch of `_physics_process` runs before the "is this a puppet?" check, so a puppet drawn as an egg falls with its own gravity and even sends position packets as if it were the local player. Not touched here. Needs a look with a druid in the match.
- If the *landing* packet itself is late by more than a tenth of a second (a bad Wi-Fi spot), the puppet can still be guessed downward for up to a fifth of a second, then it snaps up when the packet arrives. On our home network this is very rare. A fuller fix would let the puppet feel the platforms itself. That waits for the playtest to say it matters.
