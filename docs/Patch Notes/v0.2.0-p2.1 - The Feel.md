# v0.2.0-p2.1 - The Feel

**Date:** 2026-10-10 · **Phase:** P2, the feel ([[first-playable-plan]] section 4, the user's go before the sheet's verdict) · **Harness:** the P2 proofs on the PC (below); the relay harness does not apply to the new server
**Report:** the server's report lines, `bench_results/<date>/v2_server.jsonl` (now with each person's late inputs)

> In P1 your own fighter came from the newest snapshot: a press showed up about one round trip plus 25 to 50 ms later. The arrows were drawn 50 ms behind that. So you had to dodge about 6 ticks before an arrow reached you on screen; a dodge you timed on what you saw came too late. Now your input lands on the tick you pressed it, your fighter moves on that same tick, and the arrows in the air are drawn on your tick too. A headless person that dodged on the tick the drawn arrow touched it caught 115 of 115 arrows at 0, 60 and 120 ms of lag. With P1's way of drawing it caught none: all 66 killed it first. The page also has a look setting: LOW, NORMAL or HIGH, by device, with a step-down when frames run slow.

## Changes
- **1. Inputs land on their tick** (the client clock that stays ahead). Each input carries the server tick it is for (`proto.gd`). The server keeps a queue per seat and applies input N just before tick N (`_on_input`, `_apply_inputs`). A late input is applied at once, so no press is lost; when nothing came, the held buttons count. After each snapshot each seated person gets `T_OWN` (16 B + the fighter's 75 B, 30 times a second): the tick, the newest input used, the margin (how many ticks early the inputs came, the smallest), the late count, `moves_from` (the first tick after ROUND START) and the fighter's whole state (`sim.write_fighter_state`, `read_fighter_state`). `net.gd` runs the predicted clock (`ticks_now`). It keeps the margin at 1 to 3 ticks: one tick added or skipped every 4 ticks while it pays a debt, all at once for late inputs, a jump at the first sync or after a stall. The server's report counts late inputs per seat (`late 0:300/0`).
- **2. The draw delay follows your own snapshot gaps** (the benchmark's finding: the Pixel's Wi-Fi needed about 100 ms). It is the gaps' 99th percentile over the last 5 s plus half a tick: 3 to 8 ticks (50 to 133 ms), moving 2 ticks a second at most.
- **3. Your fighter moves at once** (prediction and reconciliation, `client/predict.gd`). A copy of the sim runs your fighter tick by tick from your buttons. The sim's `predicting` switch keeps the server's calls out of the copy: no deaths, no pickups, and an arrow that reaches a fighter waits there. On each `T_OWN` the copy takes the server's state and runs your inputs since again. A small correction fades out in about 60 ms; one over 120 px jumps (a new round, a death far away). Your jumps, landings, dodges, ledge grabs and shots give their dust at once, and the server's copies are dropped. Your other events (death, catch, pickup, hurt, spawn, a stomp's jump) come as soon as they arrive, not at the draw clock. The aim line, the mouse aim and the HUD's arrows use where you see yourself.
- **4. The arrows in the present** (`client/present.gd`, [[sim-server-design]] section 3). An arrow in the air now carries its exact place and speed, its age and who shot it: 21 B (a stuck one stays 8 B). The page flies the newest snapshot's arrows on to your tick with the sim's own arrow code (`sim.fly_arrows`). The fighters stand in that copy as stoppers: an arrow that reaches one waits there for the server's word (a hit, a catch, or on it goes). You stand where the prediction has you; the others move on at their snapshot speed. Your own shots since the snapshot come from the prediction. Someone else's new arrow slides out of their bow in about 40 ms, because they are drawn in the past. An arrow's stick and tink come at once.
- **5. The look setting.** LOW (T1), NORMAL (T2) and HIGH (T3). An iPhone or an iPad starts on LOW, an Android phone on NORMAL, the PC on HIGH. The lobby's LOOK button picks another, and the device keeps it (`towerbrawl_v2_look`). The step-down: in a match, when more than 5% of the frames in 3 s take over 20 ms, it goes one look down and says so at the bottom for 4 s (`[Look]` in the console). It never steps up by itself, and the step-down is not kept: the next visit starts on your pick. A gap over 1 s counts as a tab that slept, not a slow frame. `look=` stays a test look (no setting, no step-down).
- **6. Test knobs and tools.** The page: `lag=MS`, `jitter=MS` (every packet out and in waits half the lag, plus up to the jitter), `slow=MS` (every frame takes at least that long). The server: `--spots 2,3` (the first two fighters always start on those spots). `play_probe.gd`: `--lag`, `--jitter`, a clock line, `--predict` (random buttons each round and a corrections line, `--exact=PCT`), `--shooter`, and `--catcher` (`--catcher=old` draws as P1 did). `input_probe.gd`: 29 rules, three new ones: 28, a fighter's whole state goes over and comes back the same; 29, the prediction alone is never wrong; 30, an arrow flown on from a snapshot is where the server has it. (The p1.3 docs said 27 rules; the probe printed 26.) `v2_shot.py`: `--extra`, `--ios`, `--watch`, `--look-tap`. `v2_synth.py` stamps its inputs and reads the new arrows. `v2_build.sh --phase 2` starts a phase.

## Found on the way (reported, not fixed)
- **The info line's fps reads high on a page that stalls.** `?info` takes the fps from Godot's frame time. Headless Chromium showed 8 fps while its frames came about 3 a second (the step-down counts the real gaps).
- **The FIGHT banner comes with the snapshot.** Your fighter can move from its own tick of FIGHT (`moves_from`), so on a slow link you can move a round trip before the banner shows.
- **Stomps are judged where the server has the fighters.** Your fighter is now drawn ahead and the others behind, by a round trip plus 1 to 3 ticks plus the draw delay. A stomp you see may miss. [[sim-server-design]] says to measure before adding lag compensation; the sheet has a line for it.
- **The p1.3 docs said `input_probe.gd` had 27 rules; it printed 26.**
