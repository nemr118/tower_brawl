# v0.2.0-p4.1 - Server Bots

**Date:** 2026-10-11 · **Phase:** P4 = G6, server bots ([[v0.2-transformation-plan]] G6, the user's go on 2026-10-10 at 23:15 with the sheet still empty) · **Harness:** the G6 proofs on the PC (below); **not deployed**
**Report:** `bench/tools/bot_probe.gd` (rounds, the catch test, the reach test, the stand-in for a kid, a server's death maps), `tools/v2_soak_report.py` (a soak), the round lines in `v2_server.jsonl`

> The bots got a new brain. They press buttons the way a person does. They only shoot when a copy of the arrow says it will reach someone, so no more shots into walls. They catch arrows on purpose, more often at a higher level. They find their way round each arena with a route map made from the game's own moves, and they fetch arrows when they run low. The lobby has a level button beside BOTS: EASY, NORMAL (the default) and HARD. EASY bots go for each other first; HARD bots go for people. Not on the laptop yet: the play night tests p2.1 first, then p3.1.

## Changes
- **1. The bot probe** (`bench/tools/bot_probe.gd`). Rounds of 8 bots per arena, as the server plays them: length, draws, how bots die and where (squares of 120 px with a death, the busiest 15's share, `--png` maps), bots stuck 5 s, shots with a wall between a bot and its target, what each arrow did (stuck in a wall before it reached anyone, reached a fighter, caught, met another arrow), time with no arrow or fewer than 2, pickups, the brains' cost. `--level=old,easy,normal,hard`. Test modes: `--catch`, `--reach`, `--kid`, `--deaths=<jsonl>`.
- **2. The brain presses buttons** (`sim/brain.gd`). It moved out of `sim.gd`. `sim.step()` runs it for each bot before anyone moves, and it plays its seat through `apply_input`, like a person, so its shots get the aim help. Its memory is a Mind on the fighter. Bots hang on a ledge 8 ticks like a person (it was 12 to 40), so a move replays exactly. p3.1's brain stays as the level OLD: the stand-in for a kid, and the benchmark's 8 bots.
- **3. Aim: a clear shot or none.** Every 6 ticks the brain flies a copy of an arrow (`fly()`, the sim's arrow numbers) at its 3 nearest enemies, through the wrap: straight, and past 420 px a little above and below. It shoots only along a path that reaches one. It draws the bow (EASY 16 to 24 ticks, NORMAL 10 to 16, HARD 8 to 12; standing still on the ground, as a person), keeps aiming at the target, and lets go if the path is still there. Each level has its own aim error (0.18 / 0.07 / 0.025 radians), its own lead on a runner (none / half / all) and its own pause between shots. With no path for half a second it lowers the bow with a jump. A dodge never lets a stray arrow fly.
- **4. Dodge and catch, by level.** Each enemy arrow coming within 300 px is flown on 12 ticks against the bot's body. One that will land gets the level's roll once (CATCH: 20%, 50%, 80%). With a yes, the bot tries its 8 dodge ways against the arrow, using a copy of its own dodge, and dodges the way that meets the arrow first: a catch. With a no it lets the arrow come. OLD's random dodges are gone from the new levels.
- **5. Routes** (`sim/routes.gd`, `sim/routes.json`). The arena probe's places, moves and runs moved to `routes.gd`, and the probe's own output is the same, line for line. `MovePlayer` presses a move's buttons tick by tick. `arena_probe.gd --write-routes` runs every move from every place, from five starts (a pixel's remainder either way, a pixel either way). It keeps a move only if all five land on the same floor or ledge and can stop there. The fastest move from each place to each other surface goes into the route file (2 910 moves, 57 KB), with each arena's hash; the web page leaves it out. The brain plans over the surfaces and lines up on the move's start place to the pixel. It plays the move (through a ledge hang too) and plans again when it lands. A move that lands somewhere else, stalls, or whose start place someone is standing on is left alone for 5 s. A bot hops over a fighter in its way. Goals: every 2 to 4 s it hunts (its target's floor) or roams (another floor). The level sets how often it hunts (30 / 50 / 80%). While the purple walls come it moves from one safe floor to the next. Idle for 2.5 s, or standing by its target with no shot for 1 s, it roams.
- **6. Fetch arrows.** With fewer than 2 arrows the bot goes for the arrow it can reach soonest. That means one with a floor or ledge close enough under it to pick it up from. With no arrows and none in reach, it goes for a stomp.
- **7. The bots' level in the lobby.** A button on the BOTS row shows EASY, NORMAL or HARD and steps through them, for everyone, in the lobby: the JSON `bot_level {n}`, `seats {bot_level}`, the server's `--bot-level`, and the `[LOBBY] bots HARD, picked by seat N` line. NORMAL is the default. The level also sets who a bot aims at: to an EASY bot a person seems 1.6 times as far away, to NORMAL 1 time, to HARD 0.6 times (`PEOPLE`).
- **8. The soak.** The server's `--soak` puts bots in all 8 seats and starts a match whenever the lobby comes back. In every game, not just a soak, `v2_server.jsonl` now gets a round line at each round's end and a match line at each match's end. A round line has the arena, the length, the winner, the bots' level, and each death: who, by whom, the cause, the spot. `tools/v2_soak_report.py` reads one run and checks the rules (exit 1 on a fail); `bot_probe.gd --deaths=<jsonl> --png=DIR` draws its death maps.

## Found on the way (reported, not fixed)
- **Rounds are shorter with the new bots.** With 8 bots, rounds now run a median of 10 to 14 s (p3.1's brain: 17 to 28 s), so the purple walls rarely come into play: in the 3-hour soak 4 of 680 rounds ran past 25 s, and the walls killed 1 bot of 4 765. This belongs to D2's round-length question and the sheet's rounds line.
- **Deaths bunch up more** than with p3.1's wandering brain, HARD most: the bots fight where they meet.
- **A few stuck bots remain at HARD** (1 to 5 of 1 600 an arena): hunters hop between a low ledge and the floor under it, chasing a target that does the same.
- **The level button says only NORMAL.** A child may not see that it is the bots' level; it sits on the BOTS row.
- **`tbbot`** stays with the old game; in the new one the lobby sets the bots.
