# The overnight bot soak, 2026-09-08 night

Four headless bots left to play each other all night on the laptop, on v0.1.4. It was meant to be a soak: leave it running, see what breaks. Nothing crashed. What it found instead is that a round can run for ten hours, and it showed exactly why.

**Fixed in v0.1.5** ([[v0.1.5 - Bot Routes]]): the stalls came from the bot brain, which had no way up or down the tower. It follows routes now, two guards end a standoff, and `tools/soak_report.py` checks a soak. Backlog lines that point here: 4 (the logs), 20 (the jump lock), 25 (the speed burst), 26 (the late kill), 28 (no round clock); 30 (a bot pushing at a wall) is closed.

### Backlog 20, the jump lock: six `🪤 [JumpTrap]` lines

This one has been waiting since v0.0.18 for a single line. The soak produced six.

```
P4 vel=(-25.0, 0.0)  pos=(411.0, 442.0) contacts=1 floor_normal=(0.0, -1.0)
```

The jump fires and the fighter is back on the floor the very next frame. What the six have in common: every y (152, 292, 442, 512) is a one-way ledge row, the floor normal is straight up every time, and the contact count runs 1 to 3. Sideways speed does not matter (76, 295, -170, 0, 25, -25). Note how close this looks to the v0.1.4 duck bug: a body at exact rest on a floor loses a frame. Worth checking whether it is the same one-frame problem wearing a different hat.

### Backlog 25, the speed burst: one line, and it is odd

### Backlog 26, a kill in the tail second: not seen on this build

The soak had **19 round ends and zero** kills in the 1.5 s after one. Last night's human matches: 21 round ends, zero. The 18 in the file are all older: 2 from the human match on 2026-09-07, and 16 from the v0.1.1 bot run in the early hours of 2026-09-08, mostly Bot2's Firebolt and self-kills with Kunai. So it was real, it just did not show here. Nineteen rounds is a small sample, so this is not proof it is gone.

## 6. The humans that evening, for comparison

Before the soak, four real devices played each other on v0.1.4 from 22:34 to 22:47 (matches 30 to 32): Andrew on the PC (192.168.4.21, desktop Chrome), Tav on an iPhone (192.168.4.41), Am on an Android (192.168.4.74), Brooks on an iPhone (192.168.4.34). Then Brooks stayed on with three bots until 23:01.

**The pace is right.** 17 human rounds, median **46 s**, shortest 23 s, longest 99 s, no stalls at all. That is the number to put against section 6.3 of the sheet.

**Druid ran away with it.** Andrew as Druid in match 31: 38 kills, 9 deaths, K/D 4.2, with Thorns 31 times. The three Knights came in at 0.3, 0.4 and 0.6. One match and one player is not proof, but Thorns is worth watching.

**The phones jump more than the PC.** Puppet jitter mean was fine (14 to 39 px/s), but the p95 was 2 558 px/s for Brooks, 2 209 for Tav and 1 126 for Am, against 120 on the PC. Snaps were few (5 to 11) and stall stayed near 1 %, so it may never be visible. If someone says "he teleported", this is the number behind it.

## 7. What is missing

The laptop's `server.log` no longer reaches back to the overnight window, and there is no `.1` or `.gz` beside it, so the server-side lines from the night are gone. `client_stats.jsonl` kept the events, which is why all of the above was still readable. If a soak is worth this much, copy `server.log` off the laptop before rotation, not after.

## 8. What to do with it

- **Backlog 28, a round clock.** The strongest case yet. Bots aside, two humans hiding in a three-screen tower can do the same thing. A timer, or a shrinking arena, or the fighters pulled together.
- **Backlog 20, the jump lock.** Six lines, all on ledge rows. Enough to start.
- **`bot_brain.gd` does not notice a wall.** Done in v0.1.5: routes, and a detour after 4 s without getting anywhere (backlog 30, closed).
- **Never leave a soak running into a play session.** The bots held all four seats and the log shows real joins turned away with `all 4 seats are taken`. Clear them with `tbbot clear` on the laptop first.
