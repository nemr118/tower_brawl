# The transformation plan: TowerFall play, Dead Cells look, a simulation server

**Draft 2.** The user's vision of 2026-09-10 (second message) replaces draft 1 (relay, 640 x 360 screens). Open choices: [[Design Decisions v0.2]]. Research: [[towerfall-research]], [[dead-cells-research]], [[sim-server-design]].

**2026-10-10: the fast path.** The user chose to build now and tune the look later (D18). G0 is done ([[bench-8p-results]]). G1 to G4 are replaced by two shorter phases grown from the bench code: P1 the first playable and P2 the feel ([[first-playable-plan]]). G5 and on stay as written below.

## 1. The vision (the user's words)
"I love the TowerFall gameplay, but I'd like to lean towards the Dead Cells style art. I think we build a super arena for 8 people, inspired by TowerFall. Clever ledges, balance, physics of TowerFall. Keep a Dead Cells style art style. Higher res (90px) characters, so still pixelly, but not quite as much as TowerFall. Keep classes, but align the mechanics. I'd also like to switch to a simulation based server instead of the relay."

## 2. The recommendations in short
1. **Build in this order: the server first, the gameplay on it, the art alongside.** Gameplay written for the relay would be rewritten for the simulation server. The art has its own track in the 3D prototype and joins the game when ready.
2. **Measure the three big unknowns before building** (G0): phones on a Godot server over the family Wi-Fi, phones drawing 8 lit 90 px fighters, and how 8 fighters feel in one screen.
3. **One 1920 x 1080 screen, TowerFall's numbers x4.5** (section 3). Everyone always visible, wrap on all edges.
4. **A modern look of our own, inspired by Dead Cells** (not pixel-exact). Bake the fighters from the 3D prototype into 90 px sprites + normal maps, as Dead Cells did; live 3D only if the benchmark says phones can afford it.
5. **The server simulates; clients predict their own fighter; an input lands on the tick it was pressed; our own packets, no netfox** ([[sim-server-design]]).
6. **Characters are not carbon copies.** Everyone keeps the TowerFall core and the fire-and-fetch ammo game; how characters differ (classes, loadouts, loot) is open (D3). Every combat number starts from TowerFall's decompiled value, and a tuning table records each change and why (D0c).
7. **Milestones:** v0.2.0 "The Sim" is the new game on the new server with flat placeholder art; v0.3.0 "The Look" adds the Dead Cells art, the effects and sound; v0.4.0 adds treasure and class perks.
8. **The brawler stays playable on the relay** until v0.2.0 replaces it, so family nights never stop.

## 3. The super arena in numbers
A fighter's picture is 90 px tall. TowerFall's body box is 14 px tall; ours is **63 px**, so **1 TowerFall pixel = 4.5 of ours** (px/f x 270 = px/s, px/f² x 16 200 = px/s²). The arena is **one 1920 x 1080 screen**: 427 x 240 TowerFall pixels, TowerFall's own height and a widescreen width like the 8-Player Edition's levels.

| Thing | TowerFall | Ours (x4.5) |
|---|---|---|
| Body box / ducking | 8 x 14 / 8 x 6 | 36 x 63 / 36 x 27 |
| Run top speed | 1.5 px/f | 405 px/s (a screen width in 4.7 s) |
| Gravity / while holding jump | 0.3 / 0.15 px/f² | 4 860 / 2 430 px/s² |
| Jump speed; height held / tapped | 3.2 px/f; 34 / 17 px | 864 px/s; 153 / 77 px |
| Top fall / fast fall | 2.8 / 3.5 px/f | 756 / 945 px/s |
| Dodge: speed, distance, time, cooldown | 5.5 px/f, ~35 px, 0.33 s, 0.42 s | 1 485 px/s, ~158 px, 0.33 s, 0.42 s |
| Catch box | 12 x 16 | 54 x 72 |
| Arrow speed; straight part | 4 px/f; 100 px | 1 080 px/s; 450 px |
| Arrow gravity | 0.2 px/f² | 3 240 px/s² |
| Aim help / bend reach | 36 / 70 px | 162 / 315 px |
| Pickup box | 22 x 30 | 99 x 135 |
| Stomp height gap | 10 px | 45 px |
| Miasma: half the screen | 160 px | 960 px |
| Arrows before the oldest fade | 12 (4 players) | 24 (8 players) |

Collision grid 30 px (64 x 36 cells); art tiles 60 px (32 x 18). A body is about 1.2 x 2.1 cells; a held jump about 5 cells.

**Eight in free-for-all is more than TowerFall ever allowed:** its 8-Player Edition caps free-for-all at 6 and uses 8 only in teams. One-hit kills thin a crowd fast, so it may well work for family nights. Spike G0c checks it with 8 bots before any art. If it is too crowded: a bigger canvas scaled down, or teams (D13).

**Clever ledges: rules for the arena**
- Every spot can be left two ways; no dead ends.
- No spawn spot has a clear straight shot at another within 600 px.
- Ledges at jump-sized steps (153 px held), with some that need a wall jump or a dodge.
- Wrap on all four edges, with openings that line up with ledges, so the wrap is a route, not an accident.
- Chest spots in contested places, not safe corners.
- Balanced left against right, but not a perfect mirror.
- Every ledge reachable by a bot (the route probe proves it).

## 4. Two tracks
- **Game track (this repo):** one build per phase, a "go" before each. Version numbers assume one build per phase.
- **Art track (`~/Work/tower_brawl_3d_proto`):** its own sessions and kickoff. It hands finished sprite sheets to the game.

### G0: v0.1.8 "Bench" (throwaway code on a branch; nothing changes for the family)
- The 8-player benchmark: a stand-in simulation server on the laptop, 7 synthetic clients, and a phone page that climbs render tiers (plain sprites → normal-map lights → particles → shaders → live 3D → stress) at full resolution and at 1080p. Full design: [[bench-8p-plan]].
- **Proof:** `tools/bench_report.py` tables and a verdict page `bench-8p-results.md`: the server's tick time and CPU, the Wi-Fi round trip and stalls, the highest tier each phone holds at 60 fps, the 10-minute heat check.

### G1: v0.1.9 "Sim Core"
- `--server` mode; a 60-tick loop; the simulation as plain objects, separate from drawing.
- The input and snapshot packets; prediction and reconciliation for your fighter; interpolation for the others; the client clock that stays ahead.
- Lobby, seats, tokens, the rejoin grace, the version check, `status.json`, the log lines and the stats cards move from `serve_game.py` into the Godot server. Python serves the files only.
- A grey fighter box that runs and jumps in a test level, on its own port beside the brawler.
- **Proof:** scenarios `sim_join_8`, `sim_rejoin`, `sim_version_mismatch`, `prediction_error` (2 px or less on 95% of ticks at 60 ms); `tick_budget` under 4 ms on the laptop; `tbdash` shows the sim server.

### G2: v0.1.10 "The Archer"
- TowerFall's moving kit at x4.5 in the simulation: run, held and tapped jump, coyote time and buffer, fast fall, wall slide, wall jump, ledge grab, one-way ledges, duck, the dodge (8 ways, cancel, slide), wrap on all edges. All predicted.
- **Proof:** `tools/archer_probe.gd` (headless, no network): jump 153 and 77 px (±3), dodge 158 px (±8), wrap four ways, no overlap in 10 000 random ticks; `prediction_error` still passes. Sheet line: moving feels like TowerFall, on keyboard and twin sticks.

### G3: v0.1.11 "Arrows"
- Server-owned arrows with ids, drawn in the present; hold to aim, let go to fire; 360 aim, aim help, the bend; sticking, pickups (first come), head-on bounces, steals, the catch, stomps, one-hit deaths, corpses.
- **Proof:** `tools/arrow_probe.gd` (450 px straight, then the arc; the bend; the bounce); scenarios `catch_on_tick` (0, 60, 120 ms), `pickup_race`, `stomp_kill`. Sheet line: a catch I saw was a catch.

### G4: v0.1.12 "Rounds"
- One life; points to win by player count; the miasma (TowerFall's timings over a 960 px half-screen); the 1.5 s wait at the round end; the crown; autobalance; 8 spawn spots and the countdown; joining between rounds; spectators; the HUD for 8 built in code; the replay from each client's snapshot ring, with a skip key.
- **Proof:** scenarios `miasma_ends_round` (two bots that never meet: over by 52 s), `draw_last_two`, `goal_by_players`, `join_between_rounds`. Sheet line: rounds feel short and tense.

### G5: v0.1.13 "The Super Arena"
- The first real 8-player arena at 1920 x 1080, built to the "clever ledges" rules, in flat readable colours (collision shapes strongest, background soft), and a second layout to compare.
- **Proof:** a spawn-fairness probe (no spawn in another's clear line within 600 px); the route probe (every ledge reachable). Sheet: which layout is more fun, and why.

**G5: the steps (2026-10-10).** The user's go came on 2026-10-10 at 22:00, before the play night's sheet had answers. It is built as `v0.2.0-p3.1` on beta and is **not deployed until after the play night**, so the night still tests p2.1. The controls are still tuned from the sheet once it has its verdict (the last part of P2). Each step is committed on beta when its proof passes.

1. **The arena probe** (`bench/tools/arena_probe.gd`): the clever-ledges rules as checks, for any layout. *Spawn fairness:* no spawn spot has a clear straight shot at another within 600 px (through the wrap too; solid blocks stop the line, a ledge stops it only from above, the way an arrow sticks). Every spot stands with room for the body, and none sits one drop above another (input_probe rule 27). *The routes:* from every place you can stand, the sim's own movement runs a set of moves (walks off edges, tapped and held jumps with steering, run-up jumps, drops through ledges, wall jumps, ledge grabs, dodges) and records where each one lands. Then: every floor and ledge can be reached from every spawn spot and back (no dead ends); each can be left at least two ways; the ones that need a wall jump or a dodge are listed; each wrap edge carries a route. *Balance:* the spawn spots split evenly left and right, the blocks and ledges per half within 10%, and some cells differ from the mirror image (not a perfect mirror). *The bots' rounds:* rounds with 7 bots and a still person on the layout, offline: how long they last, draws, and how they die. `--png DIR` draws a flat map of each layout (blocks, ledges, spawn spots, clear lines, what the routes reach). *Proof:* run on the bench arena first; it must catch the known problem (spots 2 and 3 face each other 540 px apart).
2. **Two arenas** in `sim/level.gd`, our own designs (no TowerFall layouts), drawn as text maps (`#` block, `=` ledge, digits for the spawn spots). The bench arena stays as layout 0, for the tests that need it (the catch test, the input probe's rules) and the old benchmark page. Chest spots wait for the treasure (G10). The look stays the bench's (D18); the map pictures are the flat view. *Proof:* the arena probe passes both; input_probe's spawn rule covers every layout; the map pictures.
3. **The arena in the lobby and the match.** An ARENA button beside BOTS: MIX (the two take turns each round), or one of the two. The server sends the choice in `seats`; the snapshot header carries the round's arena (one byte); the page swaps its tiles and torches, the prediction and the arrows' copies swap their level, and the ROUND banner names the arena. The server knob `--arena mix|bench|1|2` (the tests: `--arena bench`). *Proof:* a whole match with 7 bots in MIX (the arenas take turns, 0 late inputs); the move proof exact on each new arena; the prediction's corrections like p2.1's; 8 sockets with `v2_synth.py`; Chromium pictures of both arenas.
4. **The bots on the new arenas.** The real bot brains are G6, but the night must not get worse: rounds with 7 bots on each new arena are compared with the bench arena (length, draws, deaths by the walls). If an arena traps the bots, the arena changes, not the brain.
5. **The build** `v0.2.0-p3.1` "The Super Arena": the patch page, the pictures, and the lines for the next sheet (which arena is more fun and why; I find my spawn spot; the wrap is a way round). One sheet at a time: these lines wait until the p2.1 night's sheet has its verdict.

### G6: v0.1.14 "Server Bots"

## 5. What stays, what goes
- **Stays:** the web shell, TLS, the phone controls (twin sticks), the replay, the stats cards and `stats_table.py`, `tbdash`, the harness frame (`chaos_bots.py`'s runner, reports and `--latency-ms`), the deploy and log tools, `bump_build.sh`, the sheet rule.
- **Goes at v0.2.0:** the game logic in `serve_game.py` (it keeps serving files), the relay packets (`sync_pos`, `sync_bundle`, `spawn_projectile`), deaths reported by observers, stocks and respawn, the five weapons and the specials, the tower, headless bot processes as the way bots play.
- **Dropped from the old list:** the focused read of `ws_client_thread` (the relay retires). The watching items 3, 13, 20, 25 and 26 end with the relay and `move_and_slide`, unless they come back.

## 6. Risks
1. **Netcode** (G1, G3). Prediction and reconciliation are new here. G0a measures the Wi-Fi first; `prediction_error` and `catch_on_tick` guard it afterwards.
2. **Phones drawing the look** (G0b, G8). Lights, normal maps and 8 fighters in WebGL 2 on an iPhone are unmeasured. G0b comes before any art investment.
3. **Art volume** (A2, A3). About 25 clips and 24 aim angles per class. The prototype makes a model in about 15 minutes; clips and baking are untested.
4. **Eight in free-for-all** (G0c, G6). TowerFall stopped at 6. Measure the fun early.
5. **Moving the rules out of Python** (G1). Lots of detail (tokens, the grace, the harness gate); the scenarios catch what breaks.
6. **Size.** About 11 game builds and 5 art steps before v0.4.0. The brawler keeps family nights going meanwhile.
